Package-specific review dismissals for the `quarto-hebstr-doc` Quarto extension at `~/Documents/packages/quarto-hebstr-doc`, supplementing [[feedback_review_severity_personal]]. Distinct from [[feedback_review_severity_hebstr]] (the R package). See also [[reference_quarto_lua_shortcodes]], and `rules/lua.md` for the Lua gate.

**Why:** successive walkthroughs of `filters/filetree.lua` and `tests/test_filetree.lua` produced recurring idiom, performance and duplication findings that a future reviewer will re-flag; each was assessed and dismissed on the merits.

**How to apply (do NOT re-flag these):**
- **The 7-line `kw` helper duplicated between `filetree.lua` and `add-code-files.lua` is deliberate, not a refactor target.** Each shortcode is an autonomous entry point (CLAUDE.md calls them "autonomous"); factoring `kw` out costs a shared module plus a `package.path` derived from `PANDOC_SCRIPT_FILE` in each consumer, reopening the install-depth hazard CLAUDE.md already documents twice (filetree icons, and the Typst `code.tmTheme`, which went with the Typst format dropped on 2026-09-29 by `34e7003`, leaving the icons as the live example). ~20 lines of indirection + a new failure mode to save 7 stable lines. Rejected; the copies must stay byte-identical, so keep `kw` aligned across both.
- **The hardcoded `/` path separator is a deliberate single internal convention, not a `pandoc.path.join` omission.** `relpath` is the annotation-key AND display string; the sidecar is authored with `/` cross-platform, so `pandoc.path.join` (which emits `\` on Windows) would break key matching. `/` also works for filesystem access on all three OSes. Splitting the convention only for the FS-path joins would leave `relpath` on `/` two lines away, more confusing than uniform `/`. NOTED, no change.
- **No `list_directory` cache.** Measured 7 redundant listings of 66 on a depth-3 walk (each of 7 dirs listed twice: once to type via `classify`, once to read). Once-per-render, depth-bounded, OS dentry-cached; a cache is dead metadata by the premise test. NOTED.
- **The `warn` guard (`quarto and quarto.log...` + stderr fallback) vs the unguarded `quarto.doc.is_format` is intentional asymmetry.** `warn` is standalone-safe for helper testing; `is_format` has no non-quarto behavior and a nil-`quarto` crash there is already a clear error. The `project_dir = quarto and quarto.project...` guard is semantic (project legitimately absent in single-file render), a third distinct case, not part of the asymmetry. NOTED.
- **The local `kwargs()` helper of `tests/test_filetree.lua` mutating the table it is handed is not a defect.** It writes a default `annotations` into the caller's literal, and all 16 call sites pass a fresh literal (grepped 2026-09-29). The trap a reviewer names, a shared options table, is neutralised by the defect itself: the write is guarded by `== nil`, so a reused table gets the same value on the second call and the outcome is identical. A defensive copy has nothing to consume it. REJECTED 2026-09-29.
- **A zero-occurrence coercion edge case in the sidecar reader is NOTED, not fixed, unless it contradicts YAML itself.** A fractional `depth` is the worked example: measured 2026-09-29, the static render takes its ceiling (`2.1` already shows three levels) and the dynamic opening its floor, the two never disagreeing inside one mode, and no sidecar in use holds such a value. What earned a fix in that same pass, by contrast, was every case where the hand-rolled reader disagreed with a real YAML parser on the same file, since `read_sidecar` runs `pandoc.read` over it too: a trailing comment on an `exclude` item, a flow-mapping profile, a quoted profile name.
- **The seven noted edge cases of `filters/docx-caption.lua` are decided, not missed.** Walkthrough of 2026-10-25 on that filter: eight findings, one real and fixed (a layout row of several cells now loses its column alignment, Pandoc writing it as a direct `w:jc` that outranks `Table Caption`, so one float's two sub-captions read differently aligned), seven NOTED on a measurement each: a Markdown hard break left as `w:br` (nine captions carry a break across `~/Documents` and `~/admin`, all raw `<br>`), `restyle` returning at the first caption of a block list (no Quarto shape puts two in one list, two panel floats in one document included), `top = i < #blocks`, `single_cell` ignoring `col_span` (zero `w:gridSpan` in either rendered probe), an empty caption title orphaning its subtitle (no `str_fig("")` among fourteen call sites), a bottom table caption not centred as HTML centres it (position is the DOCX rule), and `is_caption` matching a substring. Measurements in the "What the walkthrough of 2026-10-25 kept and what it left" section of the project's `.claude/DOCX-GT-WIDTH.md`; do not re-flag them without a real incident.
- **The six noted edge cases of `filters/collab-comments.lua` are decided, not missed.** Walkthrough of 2026-10-26 on that filter and its suite: fourteen findings, eight accepted (two diagnostic silences closed, ten test cases added, two trivial simplifications) and six NOTED on a measurement each, the asymmetry of `rendered_needle` between a stored form and its apostrophe twin, the counting walk not reaching inline-only containers (a `title:`, a definition-list term, a `LineBlock` line, a short caption; zero line blocks and zero definition lists in `csi.qmd`), `utf8.codepoint` raising on invalid UTF-8 (pandoc refuses such a source before any filter runs), `comment.timestamp` reaching `w:date` validated as a string only (all seventeen store timestamps ISO), a `word_id` collision dropping both comments (~7e-8 on seventeen entries, no cheap fixture forces one), and the store walk climbing past `quarto.project.directory` (bounding it breaks a render outside a project). Measurements in the "What the adversarial walkthrough of 2026-10-26 kept and what it left" section of the project's `.claude/DESIGN-COLLAB-COMMENTS.md`; do not re-flag them without a real incident, and read that whole note before reviewing the fork, whose divergences from upstream are deliberate.
- **Terse helper names (`kw`, `esc`, `opt`) are conventional Lua-filter idiom, not a naming defect.** `esc` = HTML escape, `opt` = option getter, `kw` = kwarg getter (and must stay aligned with `add-code-files.lua`). NOTED. The same holds for a module constant: `docx-layout-flatten.lua`'s `KEYS` sits beside the `STYLE`, `TYPE`, `LANG`, `PAGE_BREAK`, `CLOSING_PARAGRAPH` and `CAPTION_MARKER` of its siblings (measured 2026-10-25), and that file deals in no other family of keys, so a `LAYOUT_KEYS` rename is not owed. REJECTED 2026-10-25.
- **The decided items of `filters/docx-layout-flatten.lua` are in the "What the walkthrough of 2026-10-25 on this filter kept and what it left" section of the project's `.claude/DOCX-GT-WIDTH.md`.** Three noted on a measurement each (`layout-align` and `layout-valign` dropped where Quarto's `attr_has_layout_attributes` builds no table, `support.load_filter` defaulting to the first pass, the accessibility cost of the shapes the filter does not reach) and one rejected on a refuted premise. The confusion to expect from a reviewer is the authoring form: what bounds that filter is float promotion, so an unlabelled chunk and a labelled chunk with no subcaption are reached like a bare fenced row, while a fenced row carrying a float id and a labelled chunk with `fig-subcap` are not. A finding whose example is `csi.qmd` or `tests/docx-template-probe.qmd` is citing a float-promoted shape the filter never touches. The user declined the float handler that would close the gap on 2026-10-25, documenting the limit instead, so do not re-propose it without a real incident.
- **`depth=0`/negative clamping to `depth=1` is coherent, not a silent-coercion bug.** `scan`'s `depth > 1` guard sends 0 and 1 down the same `has_visible_entry` branch; the render does not lie. Rejected. Note: the sibling coercions raised by the same finding (`depth="abc"`, non-canonical `hidden` spellings, unknown kwargs, positional args) WERE worth cheap `warn()`s and were fixed, on the "a call site that misses the documented form should be named, not absorbed" principle.
