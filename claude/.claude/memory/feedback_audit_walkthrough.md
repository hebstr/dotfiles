## The generation check says `unverified` on calls that happened

Two causes, both measured 2026-10-08, both fixed or documented the same day. `model_matches` accepted only an exact slug or one with a date appended, so a record naming `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro-20260813` for a requested `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro-0813` came back `model_mismatch`; it now also accepts an expansion, but only when the declared 8-digit run is a valid `YYYYMMDD` whose `MMDD` equals the requested 4-digit run, which keeps `llama-3` against `llama-13` refused. And OpenRouter's publication delay is erratic: about two minutes on 2026-09-19, 5 to over 35 minutes on 2026-10-08, tied to neither generation time nor provider, with two calls three minutes apart publishing out of order, against a 300 s retry deadline that the Bash tool's own 600 s ceiling forbids extending to cover it.

So a `not_found` or a `model_mismatch` at Step 3 is never by itself evidence of a fabricated ID. Re-check the ID by a later run before rendering a blindspot report under its "external call is unverified" template: on 2026-10-08 that template held a real 0.0144 USD judge call out of the convergence analysis, and the whole pass had to be re-read once the record appeared. ## Blindspot buckets: weigh the evidence a finding needed

`audit:blindspot` frames `external-only` findings as Claude's blind spots. Measured over six runs, the bucket predicts little on its own; what predicts acceptance is what the external model had to read or run.

- **Read the judge's `**Truncated:**` line before any bucket rate, then ask what "in full" covered.** A finding the judge made about a contradiction inside text it had in full is usually right (11 of 11 `external-only` accepted on an untruncated instruction document, 2026-09-20), but the same untruncated condition gave **2 of 7 on 2026-10-10**, on `cross-model-bridge.md` passed alone to stay under the cap: the judge held the whole file and none of its contract, so four of its seven fell to a sibling file it never received or to a path convention the parent states, and one more to a sentence three lines from the one it quoted. On a single file of a multi-file skill, check every `external-only` against the parent before walking it; the reliable regime is an untruncated *contract*, not an untruncated file. **Third data point, and it settles the shape: 3 of 7 on 2026-10-13**, on `audit/sweep/` whose two files the judge held entire (24 371 of 80 000 characters, `Truncated: no`). Three of the four rejected needed something outside those two files, and none of the three was a close call: the harness's own `Agent` tool schema, the host's wording convention, and the behaviour of a spawned agent. So the predictor is not the share of the target the judge read but whether the finding's evidence lives inside the target at all; a directory target does not fix this, it only moves the boundary. A finding resting on a mechanism the judge could not run is usually wrong (0 of 8 on `export.py`, 2026-07-31: a critical on a premise one command refutes, two vectorization requests that measured at 0.7 % and 1.7 % of build time; on a rules corpus, three findings declaring installed tools hallucinations).
- **`agreed` stays the most reliable bucket** (near-perfect across runs), `claude-only` sits between. Never quote a bucket's historical rate without saying what the judge could see. On the `sweep` run of 2026-10-13 `agreed` came out **8 of 11**, its three rejections all of one shape: a pair whose two halves share a location rather than a defect, which the convergence rule then merges and which merging lets the weaker half borrow the stronger's severity. Unmerge before adjudicating, and ask of every pair what single remedy would close both halves; if none would, it is a near-miss wearing an `agreed` tag.
- **`claude-only` collapses to whatever its largest block is calibrated as.** Same run: 38 `claude-only`, of which 20 were trigger findings on an explicit-only skill, all closed as one point by pattern 4 of [[feedback_review_severity_skill_audits]]. Count the blocks before budgeting the forced L2 calls, since one calibration rule can take a third of the bucket off the board in a single verdict.
- **Accepted is not correct.** None of the eleven accepted `external-only` findings of 2026-09-20 survived as written: each needed its mechanism replaced, its scope cut or its remedy refused. The rate says where to look, never what to write.
- **Two findings sharing a false premise are not corroboration**, and a real observation can arrive with a remedy that loosens a contract the repository just tightened.
- **`agreed` is not a pass either.** On the `poc` run (2026-10-10, 39 points) two of six `agreed` pairs were rejected: one on dated prefixes the finding said were absent, the other on a taxonomy the cited rules file supplies. Both models had flagged both, which is all the bucket records, and neither had read the sentence above the one it quoted.
- **L1 and L2 diverge on about a fifth of points, with a fixed polarity.** Seven of 39 on that run: L1, the alternate Claude, argues the file already covers it; L2, the non-Claude, argues the finding. Four resolved for L2, two for L1, one on a ground neither had raised. Read a divergence as two different questions being answered rather than as a tie to break, and settle it on the file.

**The judge's 80,000-character cap drops whole files and fakes `claude-only` buckets.** The cap in `audit/blindspot/agents/cross-model-judge.md` is sized for small-context models: on `audit/walkthrough` it left the judge 2 files of 13, and eight Claude findings sat in `claude-only` only because the judge never saw their file. When the coverage line names dropped files, run a second judge pass scoped to them **before** the walkthrough, since the bucket decides L2 routing. Price it at its failure: one second-pass call (2026-09-21) looped until `finish_reason: length` for 0.82 USD and zero findings, four times the cost of a successful one; on a rules corpus, retry with a different model family. A finding whose evidence spans a dropped and a kept file stays untestable either way; say so.

**Never collapse a near-miss pair into `agreed`.** The convergence rule fires on 2 of 3 signals, and "same file and line" plus "same fix surface" reach 2 without the defects being one. Three near-miss pairs each held one real defect and one false positive on the same line. Match on the defect and the remedy, never on the location.

**A hand-written blindspot report spells the heading `### Convergence Analysis`, three hashes.** `audit:walkthrough` matches that literal string to switch on bucket routing; at `##` it silently falls back to severity-only routing.

## Running the walkthrough

- **Close the coverage check with arithmetic, not with a reading.** On the 63-row revisit of 2026-10-10 the point list came out one row short, `C5` belonging to no merged point and to no single-row point, and nothing in the run surfaced it: the rows were adjudicated in order and every point looked complete. What caught it was summing the rows each point claims against the rows that entered: 34 single-row points + 26 rows in 10 merged points = 60, against the 61 the manual bucket held (63 rows in, 2 auto-rejected in batch). Adding the missing row closed it at 35 and 61. Merging points by shared cause is what makes this possible, so run that sum before the wrap-up whenever a pass merges anything.
- **Under `--auto`, the set re-read of the authored sentences stops being optional, and the defects sit between the fixes rather than in them.** Same run: seven, none visible point by point, each created by a later point against an earlier one. A count of "two run-level anomalies" that four edits had made false; a `<reason>` placeholder reintroduced by the very point that had just required placeholders to be enumerated; an "above" pointing at a rule that sits below; two reference forms for one sibling file after a path fix had settled on one. The flag removes the pause that used to space those edits apart, so nothing else slows the authoring down. Confirmed on the second `--auto` pass of the same day, 26 points on the bridge itself: seven again, and of the same four classes rather than the same instances, which is what makes them predictable. A count the pass's own new rule contradicted (`the two tokens this branch admits` against a third form added nine lines below); an enumeration gone stale (`the file's four other strings` where six exist); pointers that no longer resolve (a forward reference to `trigger (b)` fourteen lines before the trigger list, a `those triggers` whose antecedent an earlier edit removed); and parity claims narrowed by the pass itself (a sentence naming one sibling site of a line now shared by three, a `two L1 rows` naming a section and a paragraph). **Grep your own diff for counts, for `other`/`four`/`both`, for `above`/`below`/`those`, and for every sibling file you aligned.**
- **A project whose `.claude/` is its own repository hides half the pass from `git diff`.** In `~/dotfiles` that tree is a nested remote-less repository, so a `git diff` from the parent shows the code and the suite and none of the plan-note or `DEFERRED.md` prose the walkthrough just authored. On 2026-10-10 the set re-read came up empty on the plan note for that reason, and only a second diff run inside `.claude/` surfaced its two defects, one of them a "narrowed" claim about a residual the change does not narrow. Diff both repositories at wrap-up, and read an empty diff for a file you know you edited as a wrong working directory rather than as a clean pass. [[project_claude_dir_local_only_repo]]
- **Give L1 the competing reading to attack, not your own conclusion.** A brief handing L1 both the finding and the reframing against it returned a third, better answer carrying a harness fact neither side had; a brief framed around Claude's defence got the defence echoed back.
- **Read an L2 rationale, not only its verdict.** An `invalid` whose stated reason exhibits the ambiguity the finding describes is evidence for the finding. L1 and L2 earn most by correcting the drafted remedy where the diagnosis holds.
- **A point-by-point walkthrough does not see what its own fixes change, and cross-checks validate a fix's direction, never its breadth.** Fixes interact across points (an escape hatch widening a later false positive, a rule forbidding a merge the same walkthrough performed), and the scope a fix is written with is never reviewed: on 2026-09-21 a source-file carve-out meant for one secret-pattern token was applied to all five and opened a hole on `secrets.py`. Re-read every sentence the walkthrough authored, as a set, at wrap-up, before writing the summary.
- **A rule the walkthrough writes binds the walkthrough that wrote it, retroactively.** On 2026-10-10 a point added a forced cross-provider check before discarding any finding a non-Claude model raised; two points already walked were reached by it, and one of them had no such check, the other having had its call on an L1 divergence. Running that one late cost a single call and confirmed the verdict, where leaving it would have published a rule the publishing run breaks. When a point changes routing, list the points already walked that the new rule reaches, say so, and re-run what it demands before the wrap-up; the same applies to a rule that changes what a later step must check.
- **Run `ListAgents` before a walkthrough that will write, not only `git status`.** On 2026-09-20 a peer session on the same repository rewrote four files mid-walkthrough on a tree `git status` had reported clean; nothing but `ListAgents` names such a session. Its edits arrive as harness notices saying the change is "usually deliberate", which reads as the user's doing. Check `ListAgents` before naming a culprit, and correct a wrong attribution plainly. A clean tree is a snapshot, not a lock.
